## Why It Pays for Itself

The idea is simple. If I take enough off your plate, you get to:

- spend your time on the work only you can do, the strategy and product thinking your clients hire you for
- step out of the day-to-day details and have room to think
- hand things off with confidence that they'll come back done, and done well

That's what lets EdTech Expertz take on more clients without you working more hours. Here's the math on one. Say this helps you go from four clients to five, and the fifth is the size of ClassE, $8,500 a month. At that point my fee would be $3,750, and EdTech Expertz brings in $4,750 a month more than it does today. A second client like that would make it about $12,000 a month more.

It isn't a wash, and it shouldn't be. Five clients is more to manage than four, even with help, which is why most of the new money stays with you.

## What I'd Take On

Here's what I've heard you need so far:

- In-depth research whenever you need it: crosswalks, diligence readouts, prep briefs on prospective clients, and people maps. This is the "30 people at Canvas" version, where the research comes back with more than you asked for.

Members can still come straight to you, and your clients still go through you to reach the network.
- The school-side voice in client work: sessions like Forward Edge on 10/1, messaging and language work, feedback on decks and pitches, and bringing in expert reviewers when a client needs one.
- Introductions in both directions, and help building what's next (AI enablement, L&D, associations) when you're ready.

Anything else that helps EdTech Expertz grow, send it my way. For the first month, my priorities would be prep briefs on your five parked leads, the member list cleaned up, the company cards, and the first weekly digest.

On time, I'm picturing about a day a week to start, more in heavy weeks and less in light ones. That's a rough expectation, not a limit. A short weekly check-in where you set the priorities keeps us honest on it.

Everything I build lives in your Drive and belongs to you, including the agent prompt you asked about in July. The company relationships, approving intros, contracts, fees, and the final call on positioning stay yours.

I'll never pitch members, since you've been clear this can't turn into a sales engine. My own intros stay under the August agreement, and if an intro fits both, the August agreement applies.
