## Scope and audience (corrected 092826)

**Audience:** Matt, Joe and the ClassE team. They're experienced educators who don't need US teaching practice explained to them.

**Job of the guide:** flag the words and phrases a US educator hears as cringy, controversial, outdated or unintentionally offensive, and give the word to use instead. Category, promise and pitch are out of scope; they belong in the Claims matrix and the pitch deck.

## The editing rule

Keep a line only if it tells the reader how a US ear hears a word. Cut any line that explains teaching, assessment or US school practice.

Lines that fail the rule, as examples:
- "reteach (teach the skill again)", "small group (a few students taught together)", "rostering (loading class lists)"
- The "What the teacher does next" row, which has nothing in the Do not say column
- "Check for understanding. That is a quick check a teacher does many times in one lesson."
- The accommodation vs modification row, and the formative assessment row
- The mastery rows that explain what counts as evidence ("Several pieces of evidence on one standard," "Sustained evidence over time")
- Acronyms these readers already know (ADHD, ESL, EL, DEI). Keep only the US-specific legal ones (504, IEP, IDEA, FERPA, ESSA, MTSS, TEKS).
- Legal citations in the body (Texas Education Code sections, HB 4545 and HB 1416, the ESSA history of LEP). Keep them in a sourcing appendix for Matt. In the body, a Why line is one sentence on how the word lands.

Also check the tone of principles 1 and 5. Several Say lines coach the team on how to read a question result ("One question is an observation. Mastery is a conclusion."). Expert educators can read that as being taught their own job. The word-level point (don't say "mastery" for one question, because it's a state test label) is enough.

## Recommendation

Rebuild the reference as one A-to-Z list of flagged words. Tag each word with why it lands wrong, then make that list the core of the guide. The short version is the top of the list, the principles become a one-paragraph intro per tag, and the crosswalk below goes on page 2 and in the cover email.

Confidence: about 80%. What would change it: if Joe's team mainly uses the guide to prep decks and emails, not to check a word just before or during a call, the current concept order is fine. In that case, only apply the editing rule.

### Tags, with words already in the guide

| Tag | How a US ear hears it | Words from the guide |
|---|---|---|
| Labels a child | A deficit view, or ranking kids | "weak / strong students," "a GAP student," "these kids," "competency issues," "needs help to competent," "your Tier 3 kids," "she's a pretty good student," "at risk" and "low performing" about a student |
| Outdated | Behind the times, or careless | "ESL students," "LEP," "special needs kids," "SPED student," "ADHD students," "I don't see color," "marks" |
| Undercuts teachers | The vendor thinks teachers are the problem | "teacher-proof," "make them better teachers," "finds gaps faster than teachers," "depends on the quality of the teachers" |
| The district's word | A vendor overstepping a legal or formal process | "diagnose," "intervention," Tier 1 to 3, "progress monitoring," "acceleration" (Texas), "upload an IEP," "mastery," "proficient" and "masters" for a ClassE result |
| Politically charged | Taking a side the buyer didn't ask about | equity, DEI and identity words in Texas; "culturally responsive" or "equity engine" for ClassE |
| Overclaim | Sales talk to a skeptical buyer | "proven," "proof positive," "ESSA certified," "evidence-based" used loosely, "a revolution in education," "30 personalized tutors," "meets FERPA / WCAG" before it's confirmed, "thousands of kids have better grades," "unlike the drill and kill approach" |
| Sounds translated | Fine in meaning, but marks the speaker as not local | "exercises," "an exercise platform," "marks" |

Each row in the list: the word, its tag, what to say instead, and a one-sentence why. Rows with no flagged word go. That also makes the list directly usable for product screen wording.

## Must-do before sending: the crosswalk

The guide flags at least these words from Matt's own takeaways and Joe's questions:

| Workshop says | Guide says |
|---|---|
| "Learning diagnosis and intervention platform" | Don't call ClassE a diagnosis tool or intervention platform |
| Cycle: Practice, Evidence, Diagnosis ... Progress Monitoring | Don't say "diagnose" or "progress monitoring" for ClassE |
| "One learning activity with multiple instructional functions" | Don't say "a formative assessment, a diagnostic insight and a remediation" |
| "Support any curriculum" | Don't say "any curriculum" |
| "Tier 1 support ... tools for Tier 2 and Tier 3 intervention" | No tier as a name for ClassE |
| "Acceleration (very positive framing)" | Acceleration only for the district's own program |
| "Exercise" (Matt's preference) | "Exercises" sounds foreign; say "practice" |
| "Save time for teachers" (Joe) | Don't say "It saves a lot of time for the teachers" |
| Mastery vs proficiency needs a product decision | Neither word for a ClassE result, and no product decision stated |

The guide may be right on every one. But Matt and Joe will find these by reading, which feels like being corrected. They'd rather be asked. The scorecard says 5 departures went into a separate note. Check that the note covers all nine, then put this table on page 2 and in the cover email as "Decisions we need from you."

## What I would not change

- The Why lines that describe how a word lands. For a team hearing US English from outside, these teach the reason, not just the rule.
- The framing that the Not lines come from our own calls, including yours.
- The TX/NY table.
- "If a word goes wrong in the call." It's the most useful thing in the guide for the moment it's actually needed.
