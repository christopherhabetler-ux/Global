## 1. What the email says, and what it doesn't

> "After careful consideration, we regret to inform you that we have decided not to move forward with your candidacy at this time. Our decision was a difficult one, given the significant number of individuals who are interviewing with our team."

What that tells us:

- **It points at the pool.** "A significant number of individuals who are interviewing with our team" is the only cause given. That fits what Paola said on the call: they were interviewing for a role that doesn't exist yet.
- **It was fast.** Contact came by Fri 100226. My prediction was Tue 100626.

What it doesn't tell us is whether your performance, the fit, or the numbers decided it. I can't split those causes, and I won't pretend to.

## 2. What I got wrong

**The percentage.** I wrote "roughly 75%" that you'd advance. The Dan debrief, written in the same format, said in so many words: "a judgment call with no base rate behind it, so no percentage." I broke that rule. My first draft of the Zeta debrief also had a number in it, and I raised the grade after you pushed back.

**What I did with the warmth.** I read an apologetic, personable room as a lead indicator. It isn't one. Interviewers are warm to candidates they're about to decline. You'd also told me you felt warm rooms before, and the same thing happened. The only risk I named was the role not getting budget. I should have said plainly that I couldn't tell the likelihood.

**My first read, then my second.** I first called Paola's exit line a challenge, then, after you asked me to read the room, called it praise. I can't tell which was right. Both were guesses, and I presented the second one with more confidence than it deserved.

**The rule going forward:** no outcome percentage unless there's a base rate behind it. Warm signals get reported as "warm, and warmth doesn't predict."

## 5. Prep-process lessons (what I'd change)

- **Prediction hit rate was 2 of 4.** I prepared for Dan's questions again. Paola asked about your favorite moment and your strengths and struggles, which tested values and self-awareness.
- **The prep wasn't frozen.** The call companion was rebuilt three times and the Live page was finished at 2:15 for a 3:00 call. Much of that day went to formatting, not to rehearsing aloud. The cause was upstream: the first versions had weak and short answers, put invented words in your mouth, and didn't use your Notion canon. The lesson is to freeze the materials the day before.
- **Two rules conflicted.** The Call Card said to ask your first question by minute 10; the Prep said about minute 20. Pick one and delete the other.
- **Rehearsal was cut.** The listen files and the "say cards 1, 3, 8 aloud" coaching came late. A call at this level needs two full out-loud passes with a timer, and one of them the day before.
- **Facts.** The fact check found 17 conflicts in the companion before the call. Most were fixed, but the 3% versus 46% slipped through in the live answer. A single page of "numbers I will say" would have caught it.

## 6. How to work with Chris (distilled from the whole session)

Process:
1. **Use his words.** His recorded words in Notion are the source. Never put words in his mouth. When he asks for revision, find the essence and say it back, don't just cut. When something is thin, say "give me bullets" instead of writing bad copy.
2. **Mine the whole canon first.** Notion is the data layer. Don't treat one or two files as the canon.
3. **Ask when a dictation garbles.** "Alder" for Habetler and "Kellogg" were both transcription errors that Claude carried forward instead of checking.
4. **Don't swap a format he built.** He supplied the Rojas template himself after the UI went wrong. Stay inside it.
5. **Frame first.** Answers open with a frame. Bold key phrases. Delivery cues at the start of each script. A back button on every card.
6. **Simple card layout.** Question, key points, first line, script, landing. No "do not" boxes and no core-message boxes.

Replies:
1. Start with NEXT, DONE, or NEED.
2. One recommendation with rough confidence and the one thing that would change it.
3. At most one question, in plain text. No question tool.
4. Exact paste blocks for anything he has to send or type.
5. Save to Notion and the repo. No emojis. MMDDYY dates, Eastern time.
6. Don't state a number you haven't read from a file.

Debriefs, in this order: verdict with outcome and execution grades; a table of what they asked against what the prep predicted; what landed; what didn't, ranked by cost; the questions he asked; new intel; what to do now; an honest read; delivery numbers; fact check; what the prep got wrong. No outcome percentage without a base rate.

Standing rulings (from the Verified Facts rulings file; read it before every call): never give the health reason for leaving Excel; never grade KIPP or Achievement First; Jackie is sixth grade; poetic license covers small differences like 14 versus 15 years.
