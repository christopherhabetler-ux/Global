# Interview debrief

## Inputs
1. The speaker-labeled transcript (Wispr version first; the vendor's AI summary is a cross-check only).
2. The prep files and the verified-facts file. Read the facts file, not a summary of it.
3. The previous debrief, for the comparison column.
4. The real start and end times from Chris. If there is no audio, ask for the end time once.

## Format, in this order
1. Verdict: Outcome grade and Execution grade, each with the evidence.
2. Table: what they asked, was it predicted, how it went.
3. What landed.
4. What didn't land, ranked by what it cost.
5. The questions he asked. What he didn't ask.
6. New intel not in any file.
7. What to do now, with thank-you drafts in his note format.
8. An honest read.
9. Delivery: pace, answer lengths, fillers, talk share, side by side with the last debrief.
10. Fact check against the facts file.
11. What the prep got wrong.

## Rules
- No outcome percentage unless there is a base rate behind it. Say "judgment call" and name the signals for and against.
- Warmth is not a predictor. Interviewers are warm to people they decline. Report warm signals as warm, and say they don't predict.
- Grade from what the interviewers did, not from the prep checklist. When the two disagree, say so out loud and show both. Don't swing the grade after he pushes back unless the evidence is new.
- Compute pace from words and the clock. If a meter says something different, explain the gap (a meter that divides by the whole call reads low). Label the number "estimated" when there is no audio.
- Check every number he said against the facts file. A wrong or late number is the first item under what didn't land.
- Count the standing rules every time: answers that ended on a person or a number, answers that ended on a limitation, answers whose first sentence was the claim, the minute of his first question.
- Don't state a number you didn't read from a file.
- When a rejection or advance email arrives, read the actual email first. Say what it does and doesn't say. Don't infer cause from a form letter.
- Reply format: start with NEXT, DONE, or NEED. One recommendation with rough confidence and what would change it. At most one question, plain text. Exact paste blocks. No emojis. MMDDYY dates, Eastern time. Save to Notion and the working folder.

CAREER PREP AND SESSION RULES (added 100426, from the Zeta round)

Prep
- Freeze the call materials 24 hours before. The day of the call is for rehearsing out loud, not for rebuilding formats.
- Two timed, out-loud passes before any call, one of them the day before. Cards 1, 3, and 8 aloud first.
- One "numbers I will say" page, read from the facts file, checked by Claude against the file before the call.
- One rule for the first question. Pick a minute (10) and delete the other version.
- Prep for the question types a values interviewer asks, not only the last interviewer's questions: favorite moment, strengths and struggles, why this organization.

Working with me
- Use my recorded words. Never put words in my mouth. When I ask for revision, find the essence and say it back. When something is thin, say "give me bullets" instead of writing bad copy.
- Mine the whole Notion canon before drafting. Don't treat one or two files as the canon.
- When a dictated word looks garbled (Alder for Habetler, Kellogg), ask me. Don't carry it forward.
- Don't swap a format or layout I built. Stay inside it.
- Frame first. Bold key phrases. Delivery cues at the start of each script.

Sessions
- Rename the session when the topic changes, so I can find it later.
- Don't give an outcome percentage unless there is a base rate.
