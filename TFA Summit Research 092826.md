## The 60-second phone demo

What lands:
- Start from their morning, not your menu.
- One job, start to finish.
- Data that looks like a real school.
- Hand them the phone.
- End on a question, not an ask.

Five ways it fails:
1. A feature tour instead of one job.
2. Hall wifi. Pre-load everything and keep the offline fallback and a screen recording ready. Brightness up.
3. Demo data that doesn't look like a school.
4. Two people squinting at a small screen while you narrate. Stop talking and let them tap.
5. No answer when someone asks "where does student data go?" Have one sentence ready, especially for Dismissal Hub.

## Opening lines (edited for truth)

1. "What happens at your school when three teachers call out at 6:40? I've lived that morning, and I ended up building something for it."
2. "Which region were you in? I'm asking school leaders one question this weekend: what eats your time that shouldn't?"
3. "I build small tools for the jobs nobody puts on the org chart, like coverage and dismissal. Can I show you one and have you tell me where it would break at your school?"

The research draft of line 1 said "I ran that scramble at a KIPP school for years." That's not true (your KIPP role was the Foundation), so it's changed.

## The five-reader test (doubles as the website test)

Ask three ops people and two principals you meet to look at chrishabetler.com on their own phone for 30 seconds. Then ask, before asking if they liked it:
1. Who is this for?
2. What does he actually build?
3. What would you do next?
4. What felt off, or like a sales pitch?
5. Would you want to see it on your own schedule?

Write the answers down that night. Two of five getting the same thing wrong means that part of the page gets fixed.
